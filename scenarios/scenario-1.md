Scenario Brief

Northstar Components operates a small smart-manufacturing demonstration cell. During an unattended period, the facility monitoring system recorded several physical-security events around the IoT equipment cabinet. A supervisor believes someone may have approached and interacted with the monitoring node, but no camera footage is available. Your group has been asked to reconstruct the activity using the digital evidence generated by Node 2.

Your Mission

Determine what physical activity occurred around Node 2, establish the sequence of events, and assess whether the available sensor evidence supports the conclusion that the IoT device was physically accessed or tampered with.

Expected Evidence Sources

Node 2 light sensor CSV: /mnt/iot-lab/node2/sensor-data/light_sensor.csv

Node 2 PIR motion CSV: /mnt/iot-lab/node2/sensor-data/pir_motion_events.csv

Node 2 tamper CSV: /mnt/iot-lab/node2/sensor-data/tamper_button_events.csv

Node 2 master timeline: /mnt/iot-lab/node2/logs/event.log

Instructor-provided incident window and group notes.

50-Minute Testbed Workflow

Read the scenario and record the incident start/end time provided by the instructor. Assign group roles: evidence collector, timeline analyst, recorder, and reviewer (combine roles if needed).

Observe the operating testbed and identify Node 2, the light sensor, PIR motion sensor, and tamper button. Do not alter wiring.

Locate the Node 2 sensor evidence files and master event timeline. Confirm file timestamps and sizes before copying.

Copy the relevant artifacts for your incident window to the group evidence location provided by the instructor. Preserve timestamps where possible.

Document each copied artifact in the evidence worksheet and calculate/record a SHA-256 hash when practical.

Review the evidence for LIGHT, MOTION_DETECTED/MOTION_CLEARED, and TAMPER_DETECTED/TAMPER_RELEASED events within the incident window.

Create a preliminary chronological timeline and identify relationships among light changes, motion, and tamper events.

Capture 2-4 supporting screenshots/photographs and record any limitations or ambiguity you notice before leaving the testbed.

Your group should leave the testbed session with enough preserved evidence and notes to complete the remaining analysis outside class.

Required Deliverables

Group forensic report (recommended 3-5 pages, excluding appendices) describing the incident, evidence examined, analysis, findings, and limitations.

Completed evidence collection worksheet identifying each artifact used in the investigation.

Incident timeline showing the relevant events in chronological order and citing the evidence source for each event.

Evidence package containing copies/exports of the artifacts your group relied upon. Do not modify the original evidence.

At least 2-4 clearly labeled screenshots or photographs supporting your analysis.

Scenario-specific finding: explain whether the combined light, motion, and tamper evidence supports physical interaction with Node 2 and identify the strongest supporting artifacts.

Short conclusion that separates what the evidence supports from what cannot be determined from the available evidence.

Questions to Guide Your Analysis

Which event occurred first, and which evidence source proves it?

Does motion alone prove that someone touched Node 2? Why or why not?

Does the tamper event align with other physical activity?

Are there gaps, repeated events, or sensor readings that could have a benign explanation?

Testbed Rules

The instructor operates and maintains the testbed. Do not disconnect sensors, power cables, storage, or networking equipment unless explicitly instructed.

Work from evidence copies/exports. Do not edit, delete, truncate, or overwrite original evidence files.

Record the assigned incident window before beginning analysis. The testbed may contain events from other groups or prior activity.

Do not assume that an event is malicious simply because it appears unusual. State what the evidence demonstrates and what remains uncertain.

Do not execute unknown USB payloads or attempt to modify the Raspberry Pi configuration.
